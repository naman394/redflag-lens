Honest limits and next steps

What we would fix with more time

Limits today

No human labels. AI annotators agreed strongly (κ 0.76 to 0.90), but errors they share would not show up.

Small exams. 37 risk sentences in the test and 8 reports in the backtest, so the ranges are wide.

Weak at risk type. The best model names the type with F1 0.49.

Weak one year ahead. Future Retail’s report, written before its default, scored like a healthy company.

Next steps

Have 2 credit analysts label 500 sentences to check the AI labels.

Backtest on 30+ reports, including banks and other sectors.

Build an early-warning model on reports 1–2 years before a default, comparing each year with the last.

Compare with large LLMs such as GPT and Claude on the same 600 sentences.

Every project has limits, and we want to be open about ours. First, no human labels: our AI annotators agreed strongly, but if they share a blind spot we would not see it. Second, small exams: 37 risk sentences and 8 backtest reports give wide uncertainty ranges. Third, our best model is weak at naming the risk type. Fourth, the tool is strong once trouble is disclosed but weak one year ahead: Future Retail's report, written before its default, scored like a healthy company. With more time we would do four things: get two credit analysts to label 500 sentences, run the backtest on 30 or more reports including banks, build an early-warning model trained on reports one to two years before a default, comparing each year with the last, and compare against large LLMs like GPT and Claude on the same test set.
