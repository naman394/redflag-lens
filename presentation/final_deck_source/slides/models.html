Precision: of the flags it raises, how many are real.

Recall: of the real risks, how many it finds.

F1: one score that balances both. 1.0 is perfect.

6 · Test set: 600 sentences, 37 risks, from Jet Airways, Yes Bank, TCS, Maruti and Sun Pharma

Here we compare five approaches on the same exam of 600 sentences. Quick meaning of the scores: precision means of everything it flagged, how much was really a risk. Recall means of all the real risks, how many it found. F1 combines the two into one number, where 1 is perfect. Keyword search finds many risks but almost nine out of ten flags are false alarms, so F1 is only 0.23. TF-IDF, a classic word-counting method, gets 0.60. A 7-billion-parameter open LLM, Qwen, used without any training, gets 0.56, so a general AI is not enough. Models trained on our data do much better. FinBERT, a finance-trained model, gets 0.77 and runs in our app on a laptop. Our best, DeBERTa-v3-large, gets 0.86 and finds 92 percent of real risks. The bracket shows the 95 percent range, because 37 risks is still a small exam.
