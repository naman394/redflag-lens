10 · Two-step model (FinBERT screens, DeBERTa re-checks). Neither company was in any training, validation or test set.

To check the tool on something truly new, we downloaded two reports it had never seen: SpiceJet 2023, an airline in serious financial trouble, and Nestlé India 2024, a very healthy company. We ran our two-step model: FinBERT screens every sentence and DeBERTa re-checks the suspicious ones. For SpiceJet it flagged 25 sentences as severe, and we checked every one by hand: all 25 are real. They cover 8 separate problems, like the auditor doubting the company can survive, being unable to pay 750 million rupees, a qualified audit opinion, and aircraft lessors filing insolvency petitions. For Nestlé it flagged only 2, and both are about a normal merger approved at the NCLT, so there are zero real warning signs. So it is 8 real problems for the struggling airline against none for the healthy company. The last two false alarms show exactly what to fix next: teaching the severe list that the NCLT also approves mergers, not only insolvency cases.
