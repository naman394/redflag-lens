10 · Gensol (never seen): the tool found the auditor’s adverse remark on p.187 and a going-concern note on p.184.

This is the most important slide. Instead of single sentences, we ran the full tool on eight whole reports: four from companies that failed and four healthy ones. Then we counted severe warning signs per thousand sentences. Orange bars are failed companies, blue bars are healthy. Reliance Communications, Jet Airways and Yes Bank are clearly at the top, and TCS has zero. The ROC-AUC is 0.94, which means that if you pick one failed and one healthy company at random, the tool ranks the failed one higher 94 percent of the time. If we just count all flags, the score is 0.50, a coin toss, because healthy companies also write a lot about risk. So severity matters, not volume. Gensol is lower than Titan, but it still found the two key warnings: the auditor’s adverse remark on page 187 and a going-concern note on page 184. Note that this is only eight reports, so it is promising, not proof.
