ROC-AUC with severe signs: every failed company ranks above every healthy one.

This is the most important slide. Instead of single sentences, we ran the full tool on whole reports that the model never used for anything: Gensol, which was held out from everything, and the four test companies. We used our two-step model: FinBERT quickly screens every sentence and our best model, DeBERTa, re-checks the suspicious ones. Then we counted severe warning signs per thousand sentences. Orange bars are companies that failed, blue bars are healthy ones. Every failed company is above every healthy one, so the ROC-AUC is 1.00, and the two healthy companies have zero severe signs. If we just count all flags, the score is only 0.33, because healthy companies also write a lot about risk. So severity matters, not volume. For Gensol, all three severe signs are real, a year before it collapsed: the auditor’s adverse remark on page 187, a going-concern note on page 184, and discrepancies in the stock statements it gave its bank on page 100. If we also add the three validation companies, which we used to tune settings, the score is 0.94 on eight reports; the one pair it gets wrong is Titan just above Gensol, because Titan’s report has real credit-risk signs from its IL&FS exposure. This is a small sample, so it is promising, not proof.
