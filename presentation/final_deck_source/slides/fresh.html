11 · Run on 25 Nov 2026 with the app model (FinBERT). Neither company was in any training, validation or test set.

To check the tool on something truly new, we downloaded two reports it had never seen: SpiceJet 2023, an airline in serious financial trouble, and Nestlé India 2024, a very healthy company. SpiceJet got 34 severe signs, 13.5 per thousand sentences, higher than every report in our backtest. And they are real: the auditor’s going-concern doubt, a statement that it was unable to pay 750 million rupees, a qualified audit opinion, and aircraft lessors filing insolvency petitions. Nestlé got only 5, in the healthy range. When we looked at those 5, all were false alarms: a merger approved by the NCLT, standard auditor wording about fraud, and a business transfer described as a going concern. So the tool worked, and its mistakes on Nestlé show exactly where to improve the severe word list next.
